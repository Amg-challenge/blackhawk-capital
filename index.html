Our approach

Risk decides the weights.
Not capital.

We do not try to forecast which asset class wins next. Our USD 100 million simulated mandate earns from several independent sources of return, and every position is sized by the share of portfolio risk it brings, not by the capital it uses.

Investment choices

Three sources of return, held in balance.

Global equities

The growth engine

A broad US core with a value tilt, plus developed markets outside the US and emerging markets. We want access to US earnings and innovation without relying on a single valuation and sector mix, and we prefer broad regional exposure to a narrow country call.

  • InstrumentsVOO · VTV · IEFA · EEM
  • LimitAt most half of the fund

Treasury & corporate bonds

Duration and carry

The bond sleeve separates two risks. Medium-term Treasuries bring duration, which tends to cushion the portfolio when growth disappoints. Short investment-grade credit adds carry with much less sensitivity to rates. We keep duration for its strategic role, even after a weak year for Treasuries.

  • InstrumentsIEF · IGSB
  • LimitShort credit capped

Gold & commodities

Protection against inflation shocks

Inflation shocks can hurt equities and nominal bonds at the same time. Physical gold, supported by central-bank demand, and a broad commodity basket, sensitive to supply conditions, respond to those shocks. We hold them for their role in the portfolio, not to chase a recent rally.

  • InstrumentsGLD · DBC
  • LimitEach sleeve capped

The market outlook decides which building blocks we hold. The risk budget decides how much of each. Keeping the two apart stops a short-term view from silently replacing the allocation process.

How we size each position

Capital weight is not risk weight.

Eight broad ETFs are weighted by capped Equal Risk Contribution: each position is sized to contribute a similar share of portfolio volatility, while concentration limits keep any single exposure in check.

Why this method

A rule we can explain, test and defend.

Principle 1

No return forecasts

The weights depend on volatilities and correlations, not on expected returns. For broad asset classes, future returns are the input we can estimate least reliably.

Principle 2

Correlations count

Unlike simple risk parity, the method uses the full covariance matrix, so funds that tend to fall together are not treated as independent sources of risk.

Principle 3

Caps against estimation error

Risk-based rules favour whatever looked quietest in the sample. Short credit looked unusually calm during the rate shock, so we cap it instead of letting one quiet fund absorb the portfolio.

We compared equal weight, risk parity, uncapped ERC, minimum variance and maximum Sharpe. Capped ERC is a choice of process and portfolio structure: it is not the method with the highest historical Sharpe, and our report shows that trade-off openly.

Three rounds

One strategic core, three disciplined steps.

Round 1 · Strategic allocation · Live

A strategic foundation

The capped ERC portfolio of eight ETFs becomes the core of the fund. Every later decision is measured against it.

Round 2 · Discretionary equity · November

Focused stock research

A focused set of individual stocks, chosen on valuation and on catalysts expected before year-end, sized with the Treynor-Black model.

Round 3 · Quantitative & macro · December

A quantitative perspective

A factor strategy and a macroeconomic view, added within a tracking-error budget and always assessed against the strategic core.
